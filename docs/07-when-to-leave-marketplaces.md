# 07 · When Marketplaces Stop Being Worth It

For most of year one, the answer to "Etsy, Gumroad, or Ko-fi?" is "yes." But
marketplaces are a rental, and rentals have rent escalators. This chapter
gives the signals that it's time to move your center of gravity to a store
you own — and the honest costs of doing so.

## The signals to watch

You are ready to lead customers to your own storefront when most of these are
true:

1. **Your email list sells on its own.** You send an email about a product and
   sell 10+ copies in a week without posting anywhere else. That single
   number is the whole readiness test in miniature.
2. **Marketplace fees outgrow their job.** Remember from
   [Chapter 2](02-platforms-and-fee-math.html): at $500/month the fee gap
   between platforms was about $20. At $3,000/month, the same percentages
   mean real money — roughly $70–$140/month "rent."
3. **A channel outside their search exists for you.** Your book's back pages,
   an ongoing blog, a newsletter, a friendly subreddit that likes your stuff.
   Something can *replace* the marketplace discovery.
4. **You fear their policies, not their fees.** A single suspension at
   zero-warning is the real risk of platform dependence. If your family
   income now depends on one shop's good graces, copy every asset out
   *today*, whatever you decide next.

## The math, in round numbers

Suppose a steady month is $3,000 from a mix of channels:

| Setup | Costs | You keep (approx.) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| All sales through Etsy | ~14% ≈ $420 | ~$2,580 |
| All sales through Gumroad | ~13% ≈ $390 | ~$2,610 |
| All sales through Ko-fi, Gold plan | ~8.5% + ~$7 ≈ $260 | ~$2,730 |
| Half marketplace / half own store | ~11% ≈ $330 | ~$2,670 |
| All sales via own site + Stripe | ~3% + $0.30 ≈ $100 | ~$2,900 |

The gap, at this scale, is real: **owning the storefront saves roughly
$200–$300 a month** compared to all-Etsy — *if* you can supply the traffic
the marketplace no longer supplies for you. Earning that saving costs a
weekend of setup plus 2–3 hours a month of upkeep, so it is only worth it
once the audience half of the business already exists.

## Migration checklist (keep the marketplace open)

Do not close anything. Move the *center of gravity*, then let markets shrink
gracefully:

- [ ] Set up the own-store (Ko-fi shop or Gumroad or a plain site + processor).
- [ ] Move every existing product's files, listing copy, and license page.
- [ ] Keep every marketplace shop active — they are a *discovery* channel now.
- [ ] In marketplace listings, keep only policy-safe links to a neutral
      link-in-bio page (see [Chapter 3](03-listing-copy-and-previews.html)).
- [ ] Start a monthly "new + refreshed products" email; always include the
      store link. Email is the migration mechanism.
- [ ] Re-verify after month one: fees, taxes, and payout rails working. Never
      migrate during your heaviest sales season.

## What "own store" means concretely (three options)

- **Ko-fi shop:** cheapest path, "café" hospitality vibe, near-zero setup;
  trade-off is limited layout and files per listing.
- **Gumroad:** more polished checkout, email collection built-in, still no
  hosting you control. Best when you want a real catalog page.
- **Static site + payment link:** maximum control, minimum fees; workable
  from the same build system that made *this* guide. It costs a weekend
  afternoon and ongoing care; worth it once your product count passes ~10.

Whichever you pick, the two non-negotiables:

1. **Your email list is the asset.** Marketplaces can vanish; the list is
   the one property no one can delist.
2. **Never migrate during a peak season.** Do it in January or a slow
   summer month when mistakes are cheap.

## The end state

The point is not "leave Etsy." The point is that marketplaces become just one
of three channels — alongside your email list and your own catalog page —
each earning its fees honestly. When you can name your traffic
sources and your list converts, the fee math finally matches the freedom
story: smaller cuts, no landlord, and a business that survives any platform's
bad month.
