# 03 · Listing Copy and Preview Images

A listing has two jobs: convince a stranger in **five seconds**, then survive a
careful look for **five minutes**. Words do the first job; preview images do
both. Here is how to write and design both without pretending to be a
marketing agency.

## Listing copy: the five-second architecture

Your title and opening lines get skimmed as a search-result card. Work in this
order:

1. **Title = what it is + who it's for.** "Nurse Shift-Report Organizers ·
   Printable PDF · Instant Download" beats evocative titles every time. Search
   (on Etsy especially) matches noun phrases, not poetry.
2. **First sentence = the promise.** "Print tonight, finish your shift-report
   binder by Friday." Concrete, buyer-centered, no adjectives you couldn't
   say out loud.
3. **Bulleted what-you-get list.** Page count, what each page does, file
   sizes, US Letter note, license reminder.
4. **The instant-download facts.** "No physical item ships. Files appear in
   your account immediately after purchase." Five returns died reading this
   sentence.
5. **Link-hygiene.** One link maximum, pointing to a plain link-in-bio page
   rather than a sale pitch, per marketplace policy. See
   [Chapter 4](04-licenses-in-plain-english.html) for the license page the
   link should land on.

A workable skeleton:

> **What's inside**
> - 6 printable organizers (US Letter, print at 100%)
> - Simple how-to-use page, none of the anxiety
> - Personal-use license — classroom and gift use OK, reselling not
>
> **Instant digital download · no physical item ships**

## Preview images: a six-slide carousel that sells

Marketplaces show one hero image in search results; the rest of the carousel
converts. Every product gets, in this order:

1. **Hero:** the product's cover or best page, mockup-style, with a short
   readable overlay: "6 printable nurse organizers".
2. **What's included:** every page in a grid, honestly gray-labeled. Disappointed
   buyers are review-shredders; honesty is cheaper.
3. **Inside pages close-up:** zoom one page so buyers can see the typography
   and grain of the real file.
4. **A use being used:** mockup of a page filled in, or a cheerful photo of a
   binder. Photograph *your own* desk/fill-ins only — never a model's face.
5. **License and files slide:** "Personal-use license · PDF, US Letter ·
   instant download." Serious buyers scan for exactly this.
6. **Cousin products slide:** "From the *Shift-Report* family," with two
   related items. Cheap cross-sell, zero fee impact.

### Design ground rules

- **Sizes:** Etsy's minimum long edge is around 2000px; aim 2700 × 2025 for
  the hero. Gumroad and Ko-fi don't enforce much — 1200px wide reads fine.
- **Text on images:** big, short, no tiny disclaimers; text-as-image is a
  bonus, not a carrier of anything important.
- **Honesty rule:** whatever the hero shows must appear inside. Wrong typo
  molecules on your "filled in" mockup — no flourishes on pages that don't
  exist.
- **Privacy rule:** fill variants with fake, obviously sample data ("Sample
  Nurse," "734-555-0142") so no screenshot ever implies a real person's
  information.
- **Tooling:** one Canva/Figma/GIMP template per product family. You should
  be able to dress a cousin product in 20 minutes.

## Copy style notes from the whole guide

- Warm, not hyperventilated. "Save your future self an hour" beats "MUST-HAVE
  LIFE-CHANGING."
- Screens-read first: short paragraphs, real dashes, bold key phrases.
- Write the customer-review-in-your-head test: if a buyer described this
  product to a friend in one sentence, could they? If not, your copy hasn't
  found the sentence yet. Keep revising.
