# Glossary

Plain-English definitions of the words this guide uses. No exam follows.

**Bundle** — several of your own products sold together as one listing, at a
price between 60–70% of buying them separately. See
[Chapter 6](06-seasonal-bundles.html).

**Cold traffic** — buyers who arrive without knowing you: marketplace search,
a friend's mention, an algorithm. The opposite of an email list.

**Commercial-use license** — permission to use a product inside a buyer's own
paid work (printing your organizer for clients). Always sold as a separate,
higher-priced listing. See [Chapter 4](04-licenses-in-plain-english.html).

**Copy-theft** — someone reselling or redistributing your files. Response
protocol lives in [Chapter 5](05-copy-theft-response.html); lawyers live
somewhere else.

**Cousin product** — a follow-up product built by reusing a family's parts:
the refill pack, the teacher edition, the prompt volume two.

**DMCA notice** — a formal, free "take this down" letter you can send
yourself under the U.S. Digital Millennium Copyright Act. No lawyer or
registration strictly required to send one; accuracy required *not* to.

**Digital download** — a file the buyer receives instantly after paying. No
boxes, no shipping, no coffee-ringed returns.

**Etsy** — a marketplace where its search sends you buyers you never would
have found, for prices that include listing and transaction fees. See
[Chapter 2](02-platforms-and-fee-math.html).

**Evergreen product** — one that sells year-round without seasonal pushes.
Not the same as "a product about evergreen trees," which is also fine.

**Fee math** — the percentage and flat amounts a platform keeps. Round numbers
live in [Chapter 2](02-platforms-and-fee-math.html); exact current numbers
live on the platform's fee page.

**Flat fee** — the fixed edge of a fee, like Etsy's $0.20 listing or Gumroad's
$0.30 processing. Decimates small orders; irrelevant on bundles.

**Gumroad** — a simple storefront where you bring the audience and pay around
10% + $0.30 per sale. Good for catalogs and higher-priced kits.

**Instant download** — marketplace wording for "a digital file arrives right
after payment, nothing ships." Stating it plainly prevents refund requests.

**Ko-fi** — a storefront with café energy; donations have no platform fee on
its free tier, shop sales pay about 5%, 0% for its paid Gold tier. Good list
growth for community-first sellers.

**Lead magnet** — a free small product (one great printable) exchanged for an
email address; the top of the funnel for
[Chapter 7](07-when-to-leave-marketplaces.html).

**License page** — the "what you may do / may not do" file bundled with every
product. Written once, shipped everywhere. See
[Chapter 4](04-licenses-in-plain-english.html).

**Mockup** — a photo-style preview showing a product "in use" — on a desk, in
a binder, on a screen. Built once per product family; reused for every
seasonal refresh.

**PLR content** — "private label rights" material sold to many authors for
resale. We don't build on it: license honesty and uniqueness both die.

**Preview carousel** — the stack of images shoppers swipe through on a
listing. Six-slide recipe in
[Chapter 3](03-listing-copy-and-previews.html).

**Printable** — a PDF the buyer prints at home. Highest margin, lowest
support, quickly bundled. See [Chapter 1](01-what-to-make.html).

**SKU** — "stock keeping unit," bookkeeping-speak for one sellable item in
your ledger. Digital products have them too; your receipts spreadsheet
benefits when your products have stable names.

**Scorecard** — the 1–40 worksheet that decides what's worth an afternoon.
See the [scorecard worksheet](scorecard.html).

**Version footer** — the tiny line on each product page (`v1 · Oct 2026`)
that dates your work, improves refreshes, and quietly debunks
would-be-thieves. See [Chapter 5](05-copy-theft-response.html).

**Watermark** — a light mark on *preview* images only, proving where previews
came from. Never on paid product pages; that punishes buyers.
