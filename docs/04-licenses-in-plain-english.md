# 04 · Licenses in Plain English

Every download ships with a license page — same wording, every product, one
template you write once. Its job isn't to scare people; it's to make generous
buyers feel safe and make copy-happy buyers unable to say they didn't know.
You don't need a lawyer for this. You need clear sentences, a date, and a
name.

## Start with "what you may do"

A friendly license leads with permission, not prohibition:

> You may:
>
> - Keep the file on all your own devices, forever.
> - Print copies for yourself, as often as you want.
> - Use it in your classroom or when helping your own clients (if you bought
>   the commercial tier).
> - Modify it for your own use — add your name, delete a section you won't
>   use, combine it with your own system.

Then the short "may not" list:

> You may not:
>
> - Share the file itself, or upload it anywhere (a Drive link counts).
> - Resell it, bundle it with other products, or give it away as a freebie.
> - Take pieces of it and build a competing product to sell.

Plain sentences do the restraining. "Upload it anywhere" covers the Google
Drive, the Facebook group, and the goodwill freebie pile. You do not need
paragraphs of legalese to carry that.

## Two tiers cover almost everything

### Tier A — Personal Use (most products)

Single buyer and their household. Printing for classroom or one-client use is
a common gray zone; rather than being vague, price it:

> "Teachers: you may print classroom copies. Professional organizers and
> coaches printing for clients need the commercial tier — thank you either
> way for using this with real people!"

### Tier B — Commercial Use (rare, priced higher)

For buyers who want to use your file inside their own paid work — printing
your planner for their clients, using your template kit with their team.
Sell it as a *separate listing* at 3–5 times the personal price, so generous
buyers can self-select instead of asking you questions.

> "You may use this product in your paid client work as often as you need.
> You still may not resell or redistribute the file itself."

## The template — write it once, reuse forever

```text
LICENSE — [Product Name]
Personal use license, version 1.0 · [Month Year]
Published by Asdale Dynamics, LLC

What you may do:
- Use this file yourself, on all your own devices, and print it as
  often as you like for personal use (including classroom use).
- Modify it for your own use and print the modified version.
- Print one copy as a gift for one person.

What you may not do (without written permission):
- Share, upload, resell, or redistribute the file itself, in any
  form, free or paid.
- Take parts of this file to make a product you sell.

Health note (where relevant): This is an educational organizer,
not medical, legal, or financial advice.

Questions about anything here? See the link-in-bio page listed in
the product description.

Thank you for supporting a one-person LLC.
```

Adjust the "may/may not" lines per product; keep the structure, date, and
publisher line identical in every product so your catalog reads one voice.

## Where the license appears

Defense starts long before any dispute, by publishing the license in three
places:

1. **In the download itself** — a `LICENSE.txt` (or the first page of the
   PDF) so the file self-describes wherever it travels.
2. **In the listing** — as preview slide 5 of the carousel (see
   [Chapter 3](03-listing-copy-and-previews.html)) and one plain sentence in
   the bullet list: "Personal-use license; resale not allowed."
3. **On your link-in-bio page** — the full license text per product family,
   findable by both buyers *and people who received a pirated copy*.

## Health-care-, money-, and "advice"-shaped products

Three habits keep the LLC's education-only promise intact:

- **Never call it advice.** "Organizer," "worksheet," and "explainer" are safe
  nouns; "treatment plan" and "investment guide" are not.
- **One line, every file:** "Educational material only — not medical, legal,
  or financial advice."
- **No numbers you'd have to be licensed to give.** Doses, formulas, tax
  positions: leave them blank; the buyer's own clinician or pro fills those.

## The FAQ you'll be asked anyway

- "Can I email this to a friend?" — You may print one gift copy; the file
  itself should stay yours.
- "Can my whole ward use it?" — That's the commercial tier; cheap, and it
  keeps your license honest.
- "Can I sell the spread I made with your worksheet?" — No, that's the
  "make a product you sell" line up above.
- "Do you sue people?" — No. See [Chapter 5](05-copy-theft-response.html):
  we handle copy-theft calmly, without lawyers.
